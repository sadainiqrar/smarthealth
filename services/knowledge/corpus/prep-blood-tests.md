# Preparing for a blood test

Most blood tests need no preparation. Some do, and preparing wrongly is the most common
reason a test has to be repeated. Your appointment confirmation says which test you are
booked for.

## Fasting blood test

A fasting test — usually fasting glucose or a fasting lipid profile — needs an empty stomach.
Do not eat or drink anything except plain water for 10 to 12 hours before the test. An
overnight fast with a morning appointment is easiest. Keep taking your usual medicines with
water unless your clinician told you otherwise. Black coffee and tea count as food for this
purpose: avoid them.

## HbA1c

HbA1c measures your average blood sugar over the last two to three months, so it needs no
fasting and can be done at any time of day. It is the usual test for diagnosing and
monitoring diabetes.

## Cholesterol (lipid profile)

A full lipid profile measures total cholesterol, LDL, HDL and triglycerides. Many clinics now
take it without fasting; if your clinician has asked for a fasting lipid profile, follow the
fasting instructions above. Avoid heavy, fatty meals and alcohol the evening before either way.

## Thyroid function test

A thyroid test (TSH and T4) needs no fasting. If you take levothyroxine, have the blood taken
before your daily dose on the morning of the test. Biotin supplements can distort the result:
stop them two days before.

## On the day

Drink plenty of water — it makes the veins easier to find. Wear a top with sleeves that roll
up easily. Bring your appointment confirmation and a list of your medicines. Results usually
reach your clinician within two working days; they will contact you if anything needs action.
